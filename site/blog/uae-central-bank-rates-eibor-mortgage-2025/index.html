How UAE Central Bank rates and EIBOR impact your mortgage in 2025

Many borrowers hear the term EIBOR but don't fully understand how it affects their monthly repayments. Here's how EIBOR and the Central Bank shape your UAE mortgage.

When buying a property in the UAE, one of the most important factors to consider is your mortgage interest rate. Many borrowers come across the term EIBOR (Emirates Interbank Offered Rate) but don't fully understand how it affects their monthly repayments. Alongside UAE Central Bank rates, EIBOR directly impacts the affordability of your home loan.

In this guide, we break down what EIBOR is, how the Central Bank influences mortgage rates, and what buyers and investors need to know before choosing a mortgage in the UAE.

What is EIBOR?

EIBOR stands for the Emirates Interbank Offered Rate. It's the benchmark interest rate at which UAE banks lend money to each other. The UAE Central Bank publishes these rates daily across different periods (overnight, 1-month, 3-month, 6-month, and so on). For mortgages, the 3-month or 6-month EIBOR is usually the key reference.

Example: if your bank offers EIBOR + 2%, and the 6-month EIBOR is 3.5%, your total rate is 5.5%. If EIBOR rises to 4%, your rate adjusts to 6% — increasing your monthly installment. This is why borrowers need to monitor EIBOR when considering variable mortgage options.

The role of the UAE Central Bank

The Central Bank of the UAE regulates financial stability, liquidity, and overall banking policy. Because the UAE dirham is pegged to the US dollar, the Central Bank often mirrors US Federal Reserve interest-rate decisions. When the Federal Reserve raises rates, borrowing costs in the UAE usually rise as well, pushing up EIBOR mortgage rates.

This connection between global markets and local mortgage costs means property buyers in Dubai, Abu Dhabi and across the UAE need to keep an eye on both international interest rates and UAE Central Bank policy.

How EIBOR impacts your mortgage

Your choice between a fixed-rate and a variable-rate mortgage depends largely on how EIBOR moves.

  • Fixed-rate mortgages: your rate stays the same for a set period (2–5 years), giving predictability and shielding you from EIBOR increases.
  • Variable-rate mortgages: your rate is tied to EIBOR, so payments rise or fall with the market.
Case example: on a mortgage of AED 1 million over 20 years, at 5% interest repayments are around AED 6,600/month. If EIBOR rises and your rate hits 6%, payments become around AED 7,200/month — that's roughly AED 600 extra per month, showing how even small changes in EIBOR affect affordability.

Recent trends in UAE Central Bank rates & EIBOR

In 2023–2024, the UAE Central Bank raised rates several times following the US Federal Reserve's fight against inflation. This caused EIBOR to climb, directly impacting mortgage holders with variable-rate loans. In 2025, rates are showing signs of stabilisation, but uncertainty remains — borrowers considering long-term mortgages should factor in possible fluctuations.

Tips for UAE homebuyers and investors

  • Decide between fixed vs. variable rates. Fixed rates offer stability during volatile markets; variable rates can save money if EIBOR trends lower.
  • Compare multiple banks. Each bank applies its own margin above EIBOR, so getting several quotes helps you find the best deal.
  • Stay informed on Central Bank updates. Policy announcements often signal upcoming shifts in mortgage costs.

Conclusion

Both UAE Central Bank rates and EIBOR play a vital role in shaping mortgage affordability. By understanding how these benchmarks work, buyers and investors can make smarter decisions about choosing between fixed and variable rates — and with expert advice from a trusted broker, stay ahead of market shifts.
